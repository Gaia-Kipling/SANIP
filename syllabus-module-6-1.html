Module 6.1 – International Level 2 Western Riding Coach

SANIP Certificate – International Level Two Coach (Western Riding)

Henry Grimes Syllabus

Entrance Requirements

  • Discipline specialisation is permitted at Level Two.
  • Successful completion of Modules 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5; minimum assessment age is 20 years.
  • At least 75 logged hours of lessons taught in the nominated competition discipline(s), submitted with the application at least one month before assessment.
  • At least 75 logged hours of personal competitive riding in the nominated discipline(s), submitted at least one month before assessment.
  • At least 15 hours worked at a recognised show for each nominated competition discipline; provincial/national judges in the relevant discipline are exempt.
  • Evidence of the candidate’s and pupils’ competition achievements.

Certification Information

  • Minimum pass rate for all subjects: 70%.
  • A recognised Level One First Aid Certificate is required for certification.

Western Riding Level Two

Candidates ride two tacked-up horses. The Level One riding criteria must be more consolidated, with a secure and supple position in all three gaits. Candidates must assess each horse, develop its work, address basic faults, and show shortening and lengthening of stride, transitions, lateral work, counter-canter, flying changes and turn-around on the hind leg. They must understand progressive training principles and discuss the horses’ way of going in relation to the scales of training.

Western Teaching Assessments – Level Two

The candidate must teach an individual and a group to an elementary Western riding standard. The assessment includes developing lateral work and simple changes, two-track transitions, pivots, speed control and 180-degree turns. Candidates must demonstrate the ability to train basic gaits, deal with problems, motivate and plan for riders, assess progress, set appropriate goals and give useful feedback and praise.

The practical work includes simple and flying changes, circles with speed control, spins and stops, together with the ability to discuss the work performed and its effect on the horses.

Level Two assessment syllabuses for Vaulting, Tourism, other competition disciplines and Driving are available from the managing director.
