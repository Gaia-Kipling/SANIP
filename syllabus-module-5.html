Module 5 – International Level 1 Coach

SANIP Certificate – International Level One Coach

Henry Grimes Syllabus

Entrance Requirements

  • Grade 12 (matric) school leavers certificate.
  • Successful completion of Modules 1, 2, 3 and 4.
  • Minimum assessment age: 18 years.

Certification Information

  • The minimum pass rate for all subjects in this module is 70%.
  • For certification, candidates must hold a recognised Level One First Aid Certificate and a Grade 12 school leavers certificate, or equivalent.

Subject Knowledge and Skills

  • 5.5.1 Personal Riding – flatwork and jumping.
  • 5.5.2 Individual Lesson on the Flat.
  • 5.5.3 Group Jumping Lesson.
  • 5.5.4 Theory of Teaching 2.
  • 5.5.5 Portfolios of Evidence – riding logs and teaching/lesson logs.

Assessments are generally 45 minutes, except for the written Theory of Teaching assessment, which is one hour and forty-five minutes.

Personal Riding

The flatwork assessment requires the candidate to ride and assess an unknown horse to novice dressage level, demonstrate an effective and sympathetic balanced seat, use coordinated aids, ride transitions and lateral work, and demonstrate preparation for school movements including leg-yield, figures of eight and serpentines.

The jumping assessment includes walking and understanding a course, warming up a known horse, jumping a course of six show jumps to a maximum height of 90 cm, maintaining an effective and sympathetic position, dealing appropriately with refusals and run-outs, and demonstrating an understanding of rhythm, pace and striding.

Lesson on the Flat

The candidate must be able to assess horse and rider, identify faults, give clear instructions, improve the combination, work with novice dressage requirements, develop training plans and communicate effectively. Lesson content includes areas such as lengthened trot/canter, accuracy, circles, serpentines, leg yielding, centre line and halt, giving and retaking the reins, and preparation for transitions.

Jumping Lesson

The candidate must demonstrate the ability to teach and control a group of three to four riders, assess horses and riders, identify and correct faults, apply current training principles for jumping up to 85 cm, deal sympathetically with problems, provide constructive feedback and introduce new skills using the IDEAS principles: Introduce, Demonstrate, Explain, Attend and Summarise.

Theory of Teaching

The written assessment covers competition rules, the scales of training, safety of horse, rider and equipment, suitability of horses for pupils, solving problems relating to the horse’s way of going, accurately marking out a dressage arena, and constructing jumps and gymnastics for lessons.

Portfolios of Evidence

  • Personal Riding Log.
  • Lessons/Teaching Log.

Candidates must maintain records of riding and teaching experience and should use the EQASA Required Documents Checklist for the prescribed evidence and hours.
